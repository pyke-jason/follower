# Risk Management & Consistency

Process-oriented reference for risk management, position sizing philosophy, win rate requirements, handling losing streaks, and building consistency. Synthesized from the r/RealDayTrading wiki.

---

## The "1% Rule" and Why It Is Wrong

The standard advice -- "never risk more than 1-2% of your account per trade" -- is explicitly rejected by HSeldon2020 and OptionStalker. Here is why:

### The Problem with Percentage-Based Risk
- A $50,000 account with a 2% rule means exiting at a $1,000 loss regardless of the chart.
- This ignores the stock's technical structure entirely. Your exit is based on your P&L, not on whether the thesis is invalidated.
- Institutions know where retail stops cluster and their algorithms are designed to trigger them.
- If a $100 stock pulls back to $99 but VWAP is at $98.50 and the daily chart is strong, exiting at $99 because you hit "1%" throws away a winning trade.

### What Risk Actually Means
You are only risking the distance to your stop. Buying 1,000 shares of AAPL at $160 with a mental stop at $159 means you are risking $1,000 -- not $160,000.

### What Determines Your Stop
Your stop is determined by the CHART, not your account balance:
- Where is the nearest technical support/resistance?
- Where does the thesis get invalidated?
- At what price does relative strength/weakness break down?

Then size your position so that the dollar amount at that technical stop is something you can tolerate emotionally and financially.

## Risk/Reward Ratios: Should You Use Them?

**For stock day trades and swing trades: No.** (HSeldon2020)

### Why R:R Fails for Retail Traders
- To calculate R:R, you need to know the probability of each outcome. New traders have no idea what the probability is.
- Setting a stop at $99 and a target at $101 on a $100 stock assumes equal probability of each. It almost never is.
- In practice, "risk/reward" becomes watching your P&L and panicking when you are down. The math goes out the window.

### What Experienced Traders Actually Do
They make a binary (yes/no) decision: "Has the thesis been invalidated or not?"
- If the stock lost RS, broke below support, or the market changed: EXIT.
- If the thesis is intact but the stock pulled back: HOLD.
- No ratio calculation needed. The decision is: is this still a good trade or not?

### Where R:R IS Useful
- **Option spreads.** A bullish put spread with $1 credit on a $5-wide spread gives 25% ROI. You need to win more than 80% of the time. This is calculable.
- **Butterflies.** A $2 butterfly with $20 max gain gives 10:1. You need the stock to hit the center strike on expiration 10% of the time.

### The Real Framework
Instead of pre-trade R:R, use post-hoc analysis from your journal:
- What is your actual win rate for a given setup?
- What is your average winner vs. average loser?
- Does the math work? If not, either improve your entries, raise your targets, or stop using that setup.

## Win Rate: Why It Matters More Than Profit Factor

### The Math (HSeldon2020)

**80% win rate, 20 trades/day, $500 daily goal:**
- Win 16, lose 4.
- Need only $50/win, can tolerate $75/loss.
- On a $100 stock: target $100.50, stop at $99.25. Wide room. High flexibility.

**50% win rate, 20 trades/day, $500 daily goal:**
- Win 10, lose 10.
- Need $100/win, can only lose $50/trade. That is a 2:1 profit ratio requirement.
- Much tighter stops, fewer qualifying setups, less room for error.

**20% win rate, 20 trades/day, $500 daily goal:**
- Win 4, lose 16.
- Need $500/win, can only lose $93.75/trade. 5.3:1 profit ratio required.
- You must find setups that deliver 5x returns. On most days, only low-float gappers qualify. These are the most volatile, unpredictable trades in existence.
- The chance of two consecutive losing days is 10.9%. This will blow your monthly goal regularly.

### What a High Win Rate Gives You
1. **Fewer trades needed per day.** You can be more selective.
2. **Lower profit target per trade.** Less pressure on each position.
3. **Higher loss tolerance per trade.** Your stops can be wider, letting trades breathe.
4. **Psychological stability.** Confidence builds on winning. Constant losing destroys even experienced traders.
5. **Income predictability.** At 80%+ win rate, your monthly income becomes projectable within a small error range.

### The Target: 75% Win Rate
Trade 1 share until you hit 75%+ for multiple consecutive months (OptionStalker). Only then start thinking about scaling up. This takes most people 1-2 years.

## How to Be Consistently Profitable

### The Three Most Important Rules (HSeldon2020)

**1. Don't Anticipate -- Confirm.**
Stop trying to catch moves before they happen. Set alerts, draw trendlines, wait for the break. You will miss the very beginning of the move, but you will avoid dozens of false starts.

**2. Trade the Chart, Not the P&L.**
If you are exiting because you are "down too much" rather than because of a technical violation, your position is too large. Trade as if you have 1 share. Make decisions based on price action alone.

**3. Market First, Stock Second.**
Always know what SPY is doing. Are you shorting into a bullish day? Going long into a red day? Is the stock strong/weak relative to the market? If SPY reverses, your trade will likely be impacted.

### The Process for Consistency

1. **Journal every trade.** Use TraderSync or equivalent. Tag each trade with the setup used.
2. **Calculate your stats.** Win rate, average win, average loss, profit factor. Track by setup type.
3. **Identify what works.** Which setups produce the best results? Which time of day? Which market conditions?
4. **Eliminate what doesn't.** If a setup has a sub-60% win rate over 100+ trades, stop using it or refine it.
5. **Project your income.** At stable stats: (win rate x avg win x trades/day) - (loss rate x avg loss x trades/day) = daily expected profit. This becomes your "salary."
6. **Every six months, increase your base.** HSeldon2020 increases his account base by 15% every six months (32.25% annually compounding).

### Walk-Away Analysis
After each day, look at the trades you exited and check where the stock ended up 1 hour later and at end of day. You will consistently find that you left money on the table on winners. This analysis builds the emotional discipline to let trades run.

## Handling Losing Streaks

### Recognize the Problem
Most traders have occasional huge losses but no equally huge wins. This is because:
- You HOPE losers will turn around (blind faith in a losing thesis).
- You FEAR winners will reverse (no faith in a winning thesis).
- Result: you hold losers too long and cut winners too short.

### The Fix: Balance
If you are willing to sit through a $2 drawdown on a loser, you must be willing to sit through a $2 run on a winner. The easiest way to improve your P&L is not eliminating big losses (that is hard) but ADDING big wins by having equal faith in your winners.

### When a Streak Hits
1. Reduce position size immediately. Do not try to "make it back" with larger trades.
2. Go back to 1 share if needed. There is no shame in this.
3. Review your journal. Are you forcing trades? Trading in chop? Ignoring the market?
4. Check: are your setups still valid in current market conditions? Bear markets and chop environments reduce the reliability of many setups.
5. Do NOT stop trading entirely (unless you are hemorrhaging capital). Keep trading 1 share to maintain your skills and data collection.
6. Focus on process, not outcomes. If your process is correct, the results will come.

### What Not to Do
- Do not increase size to recover losses.
- Do not switch to untested strategies.
- Do not trade out of frustration, boredom, or FOMO.
- Do not abandon your journal.

## Growing Small Accounts

### The PDT Problem
Accounts under $25,000 are limited to 3 day trades per rolling 5-day period. This constrains the RDT method significantly.

### Specific Strategies for Small Accounts

**1. Swing trades as the primary vehicle.**
With limited day trades, focus on D1 setups that you hold 2-5 days. Use your day trades only for the highest-probability, most time-sensitive opportunities.

**2. Algo price point breaks.**
Draw algo lines on 50+ stocks. Set alerts. When a price point is breached with confirmation, enter near end of day and swing. HSeldon2020 specifically designed this method for sub-PDT accounts.

**3. Options spreads.**
- Bullish put spreads: sell OTM puts below technical support. Collect 20%+ credit per dollar of spread width. Time decay works in your favor.
- Call debit spreads: cap your risk while maintaining upside exposure.

**4. Futures (micro contracts).**
/MES (micro S&P) has no PDT restrictions. It is pure technical analysis. Skills transfer directly to stock trading. Multiple wiki contributors recommend this for sub-$25K accounts.

**5. Cash account alternative.**
Trade in a cash account (no margin). Trades settle T+1. You can make unlimited day trades but must wait for settlement. Requires careful capital rotation.

### The Psychological Challenge
Small accounts create pressure to "make it back" or "grow fast." This leads to oversizing, overtrading, and abandoning the method. The counter-intuitive truth: trade SMALLER until your win rate proves you deserve to size up. Keep your "tuition" low. Most of the money lost by new traders is lost before they learn the skill.

### What $5K/Month Looks Like
HSeldon2020 ran a public $5K challenge, doubling the account in 5 weeks with no momentum trades. The method works at any account size -- the mechanics are the same. Only the position size changes.

## The Walk-Away Framework

After each trading session:

1. For every trade you exited, record where the stock was 1 hour later and at EOD.
2. For trades you did NOT take (watched but passed), record what happened.
3. Calculate your "walk-away profit factor" -- what your P&L would have been if you held all winners to EOD.
4. Compare this to your actual P&L.

The gap between actual and walk-away P&L reveals exactly how much money you leave on the table by cutting winners short. Over time, this analysis will change your behavior more than any rule or guideline.
