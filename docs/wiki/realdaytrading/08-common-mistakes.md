# Common Mistakes & How to Fix Them

Most trading mistakes are not technical failures. They are psychological patterns wearing technical disguises. You shorted against a strong market? That is counter-trend trading -- a mindset issue. You loaded up on OTM calls before earnings? That is gambling -- a mindset issue. You averaged down on a stock that kept dropping? That is fear of loss -- a mindset issue.

This document catalogs the most common mistakes, diagnostic frameworks for identifying your specific problems, and actionable methods to fix them.

---

## The 8 Types of Trading Screw-Ups

Every mistake a trader makes falls into one of these categories:

1. **Trading without knowledge.** Entering positions you do not understand. Buying option spreads without knowing how to leg out. Asking what "assignment" means after buying a same-day call.

2. **Rushing into trades.** A stock is moving, someone posted a trade, and you jump in without checking charts, identifying support/resistance, or deciding on position size. FOMO in its purest form.

3. **Relying on others.** Blindly following someone else's trades means you also depend on them for exits. "Are you still in?" is the hallmark question of a trader who does not own their position.

4. **Position sizing errors.** A $15K account buying 30 contracts on a single stock. Swinging for the fences when you should be hitting singles.

5. **Gambling with OTM options.** They are cheap for a reason -- you are paying 100% premium with zero intrinsic value. They require massive stock moves to profit and bleed to theta constantly.

6. **Anticipating instead of confirming.** Trying to catch tops and bottoms. Going long because a stock "has to bounce." Buying before earnings because you "know" it will beat. Successful traders do not predict the market -- they act when the market confirms a direction.

7. **Overthinking.** So many indicators on the chart you cannot see the candles. The 73 SMA on the 4-hour chart. Technical analysis works because other traders follow the same guides. Nobody is looking at your esoteric indicator.

8. **Believing in losers, scared of winners.** Holding losers because "it has to turn around" while cutting winners because "the other shoe will drop." Your losers become bigger than your winners. This single pattern destroys more accounts than any other.

## 4 Easily Fixable Mistakes

These mistakes require no deep psychological work -- just discipline:

### 1. Stop gambling with OTM options

OTM options have no intrinsic value. You are paying pure premium. The math is clear:

Take a stock at $58.22. Spend $500 on either:
- **2x ITM calls** ($56 strike, $2.35 each): If the stock barely moves, you lose $26. If it goes up $1, you profit $174.
- **16x OTM calls** ($59 strike, $0.30 each): If the stock barely moves, you lose $480 (total wipeout). If it goes up $1, you still lose $128.

**Three rules that will immediately improve your options trading:**
1. Do not hold options over earnings.
2. Choose calls/puts with delta of 0.6 or higher, at least a week out.
3. Do not trade spreads unless you know how to leg out of them.

### 2. Stop ignoring the market

If SPY is ripping higher and you are shorting individual stocks, you are fighting the tide. Always have the SPY 5-minute chart up. Get the market right and the stock right -- that is the formula.

### 3. Stop trading your P&L

Your exits should be based on charts, not on how much you are up or down. Jumping out because you are "down too much" (see: position sizing) or because you want to "lock in" a gain leads to cutting winners and holding losers.

### 4. Stop rethinking the wheel

You have been trading three months and think you cracked the code? You have not. Find one other person who became consistently profitable without putting in years of hard work. When you cannot, accept that you are not the exception.

## Analysis Paralysis -- How to Break Through

Traders gravitate toward one of two extremes:

**The Overthinker:** Waits for perfect alignment of every indicator. Pullback to 8EMA, 3/8 cross, confirmation candle, bullish SPY, RS on stock, good volume, strong daily chart. By the time everything lines up, the move is over or noise triggers a premature exit.

**The Oversimplifier:** Sees red candles, sees market down, immediately shorts. No support/resistance check, no algo lines drawn, no story understood.

**The solution:** Err toward overthinking when learning, but add one critical question to your checklist: "What is the story of this stock right now?"

Not every checkbox needs to be green. Variables have different weights. A weak daily chart can be overcome by multiple bounces off major support, high relative volume, relative strength, and a clear stop. A strong daily chart can be negated by trading against the market direction. Learn to weigh the variables, not just count them.

## Win Rate Is Your Most Important Number

Here is the math that proves it. Consider two traders:

**Person 1:** 60% win rate, average winner $300, average loser $300.
- Per 100 trades: 60 wins x $300 = $18,000 in gains. 40 losses x $300 = $12,000 in losses.
- Net profit: $6,000.

**Person 2:** 60% win rate, average winner $150, average loser $450. (Cuts winners, holds losers.)
- Per 100 trades: 60 wins x $150 = $9,000 in gains. 40 losses x $450 = $18,000 in losses.
- Net loss: -$9,000.

Same win rate. One makes $6,000, the other loses $9,000. The difference is entirely in the ratio of average win to average loss.

Now consider the effect of just three mindset mistakes per week on a trader averaging 20 trades per week with a 75% win rate:

- 0 mistakes: 75% WR, 15 wins, 5 losses. At $300 avg: +$3,000/week.
- 3 mistakes (turning winners into losers): 60% WR, 12 wins, 8 losses. At $300 avg: +$1,200/week.

Three mistakes per week -- one moment of tilt, one FOMO entry, one held loser -- costs you 60% of your weekly profit.

**The takeaway:** Win rate matters, but the profit factor (total gains / total losses) matters more. You need both a decent win rate AND discipline to let winners run and cut losers quickly.

## Walk-Away Analysis

This is the single most important analytical exercise a trader can do. It reveals exactly how much money you are leaving on the table by exiting too early, and whether you are picking stocks with staying power.

### How to Do It

At the end of each trading day, open a spreadsheet with these columns:

| Column | Contents |
|--------|----------|
| A | Ticker |
| B | Long or Short |
| C | Entry price (stock or option) |
| D | Price at market close |
| E | Profit/Loss if held to close |

Fill in every trade you took that day. Add up Column E for your "walk-away" total.

### What to Compare

1. **Is the walk-away total higher than your actual P&L?** If yes, you are picking good stocks but exiting too early. Your trade selection is sound; your patience is not.

2. **What is the walk-away win rate vs. your actual win rate?** If your walk-away win rate is below 50%, you are not picking the right stocks. If it is significantly above your actual win rate, you are being shaken out of winners by intraday noise.

3. **What is the walk-away average win vs. average loss compared to your actuals?** This shows whether your exits are systematically cutting profits short.

### Expanded Version

For deeper analysis, add time-interval columns:

| Time | What to record |
|------|---------------|
| 5 min after exit | Price at that point, would-be P&L |
| 1 hour after exit | Price at that point, would-be P&L |
| End of day | Price at close |
| End of next day | Price at next day's close |

Compare win rate and profit factor at each interval. This reveals your optimal hold time.

### What It Tells You

- **Walk-away P&L higher than actual:** You are exiting winners too early. The intraday movement scaring you out of trades is mostly noise.
- **Walk-away P&L lower than actual:** You are picking momentum-only stocks with short windows of profitability. You need to find stocks with better daily charts and staying power.
- **Walk-away win rate much higher than actual:** You are getting shaken out of good positions. The stocks you pick do go your way -- you just are not there to see it.

Do this exercise weekly for at least a month. Most traders find they are leaving significant money on the table.

## The "I'm Still Not Profitable" Diagnostic Checklist

If you are not consistently profitable, run through this checklist honestly. Check every item that applies:

- [ ] You are more likely to go long on a stock that just dropped than one that just went up
- [ ] You average down more often than you average up
- [ ] You almost never average up
- [ ] You leave positions either way too quickly or way too slowly
- [ ] Your losses are significantly larger than your winners
- [ ] At least once a week you exit positions out of impatience
- [ ] At least once a week you exit because you are "losing too much money"
- [ ] Your position sizes are clearly designed for big wins
- [ ] You get stuck in positions so deep in the red you cannot bear to close them
- [ ] At least once a week you spend hours staring at one chart hoping it turns around
- [ ] At least once a week you make a FOMO trade
- [ ] More than half your trades are against the market direction
- [ ] You are constantly betting on reversals
- [ ] You keep adding new indicators or trying new methods
- [ ] You start the day following your method but abandon it by afternoon
- [ ] You follow others' trades and depend on them for exits
- [ ] At least once a week you make a trade you do not fully understand
- [ ] Your profits are consistently wiped out by one big loss
- [ ] Your confidence swings between overconfidence and no confidence
- [ ] When in profit, your first thought is "close and take the win"

**Every single item above is a mindset issue.** More knowledge, more indicators, more chart study will not fix any of them. You need to address the psychological root cause.

**The core diagnostic:** It does not take two years to learn the method. It takes two years to change your mindset. If you are not following the 10-step process and putting in the deep work on psychology, you will not become consistently profitable. There are no shortcuts.

## How to Analyze and Log Trades

### The 3 Categories of Losing Trades

Every losing trade falls into one of these buckets:

**Category 1: Execution error.** You broke your own rules. Entered without confirmation, sized too large, held past your stop, traded against the market. These are fixable with discipline.

**Category 2: Correct trade, wrong result.** Your analysis was sound, execution was clean, but the trade fell on the wrong side of probability. With a 75% win rate, 1 in 4 trades will lose -- that is expected. Do not let these shake your confidence.

**Category 3: Missed information.** You did not see the algo line, missed the sector rotation, ignored a pending news catalyst. These are fixable with more thorough pre-trade analysis.

### Self-Sabotage Diagnostic (Standard Deviation Method)

Export all your trades to a spreadsheet. Calculate:
- **Mean P&L per trade**
- **Standard deviation of P&L per trade**

**Healthy profile:** Mean +$85, SD $80. Tight distribution. You are consistent with occasional small outliers.

**Unhealthy profile (type 1):** Mean +$85, SD $80, but with 3-5 massive negative outliers more than 4 SDs below the mean. You are a solid trader who periodically goes off the rails. Fix: identify the trigger for blowup days and walk away when you see it.

**Unhealthy profile (type 2):** Mean +$200, SD $500. Wide swings in both directions. You take oversized positions constantly. Fix: cut average position size in half. Only scale to full when halfway to target.

### The Emotion Column

Add an emotion column to your journal. Note how you felt during each trade: confident, anxious, fearful, greedy, impatient, angry. After a month, correlate emotions with outcomes. You will find that your worst losses cluster around specific emotional states. Those states are your triggers.

## Three Archetypal Mistakes (The FAFO / GTFO / GFY Framework)

### Mistake 1: The Betrayal (FAFO)

You enter a good trade. It goes against you, then comes back to breakeven. You exit at scratch because the trade "betrayed your trust." It then runs to your original target without you. You no longer trusted the trade because it caused you emotional pain. The trade did nothing wrong -- it tested support and bounced exactly as expected.

**Fix:** Your thesis was working. Ask: "Has my reason for entering changed?" If no, stay in.

### Mistake 2: The Hope Trade (GTFO)

You enter a good short with perfect analysis. News hits and the market reverses. You are down big but the position is so large you cannot stomach the loss. You hold overnight on hope. The next day it gaps against you further. You close for a devastating loss. All of your analysis was reduced to hope because your position was too large to exit rationally.

**Fix:** If this had been half the position size, you would have closed at the first sign of trouble. Size so that the worst-case loss is tolerable.

### Mistake 3: The Quick Profit (GFY)

You go long a stock at an all-time high after strong earnings. It breaks out of consolidation on volume. Everything is perfect. It pops $1 and you immediately exit, proud of your win. The stock runs another $3 without you. You see the market as a casino and cashing in any win as beating the house.

**Fix:** Winning should be the expectation, not a lucky break. When everything is working perfectly, that is when you hold and add -- not when you grab $1 and run.

## Gambling with OTM Options -- Why It Fails

The appeal: they are cheap. If the stock explodes, they multiply.

The reality: you are betting on a massive stock move that must happen fast enough to outrun theta decay. With low delta, the option price barely moves even when the stock does. You need the stock to run through your strike and keep going just to break even.

The math always wins. If every new trader calculated what their account would look like with OTM option trades removed, they would be shocked at how much they have lost to this single habit.

**The three rules:**
1. Delta of 0.6 or higher.
2. At least one week until expiration.
3. Never hold options over earnings announcements.

## Trading with Fear vs. Trading with Confidence

Fear and confidence are not personality traits in trading -- they are the result of process.

**Fear comes from:**
- Trading money you cannot afford to lose
- Position sizes so large that one bad trade causes real financial damage
- No proven edge -- your results feel random (like gambling)
- No statistical track record to lean on

**Confidence comes from:**
- A journal with thousands of trades proving your edge
- A win rate and profit factor achieved consistently over months
- Position sizes where the worst-case loss is insignificant
- Decisions based on repeatable criteria, not emotion

**The path from fear to confidence:** Paper trade until you hit 75% win rate and 2+ profit factor for three consecutive months. Then trade one share. Not 4 or 5 "to make it feel real." One share. Do this until your statistics hold with real money. Then slowly scale up.

You can tell your brain it should not be afraid. It will not listen. But you can build an evidence base so overwhelming that fear has no argument left to make. That is what the two-year process is for.
