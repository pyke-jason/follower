# Getting Started: Prerequisites & Foundations

## Realistic Expectations

Set these before you open a broker account:

- **Timeline: 2+ years minimum.** This is the average across successful traders who followed this methodology. Some take less, some take more. Do not compare yourself to anyone.
- **No shortcuts exist.** Every "system" or "guru method" promising fast results is either a scam or an incomplete picture. Traders who try to cut corners or chase multiple methods simultaneously almost universally fail to reach consistency.
- **Early success is misleading.** Initial wins, especially in a bull market, do not mean you have learned the skill. The market will humble you. Budget emotionally for that.
- **You will lose money when you start trading real money.** This is expected. The point of the progression below is to keep those losses small and survivable.

The catch-22: you need two years to become financially independent through trading, but you need financial stability to take those two years. Treat this like putting yourself through school while working. If you cannot trade during market hours, study and review at night.

## What You Must Unlearn

Before starting, accept that most of what you have absorbed about trading is either wrong or incomplete:

- **Discard:** Volume Profiles, Elliott Waves, Fibonacci lines, RSI, and "inverse head and shoulders" patterns as primary decision tools. If 90% of traders use the same information and 90% still lose money, that information does not provide an edge.
- **"Buy Low, Sell High" is backwards for active trading.** The correct frame is "Buy High, Sell Higher" -- enter with the trend, buy strength, and add to winners. Trying to pick bottoms is how accounts die.
- **"Nobody ever went broke taking a profit" is false.** If your wins are smaller and less frequent than your losses, you absolutely go broke. Most traders exit winners too early and let losers run too long.
- **Hard stops are training wheels.** Professional traders overwhelmingly use mental stops for flexibility. You will start with hard stops and transition later once you have 2+ years of experience.
- **The market is not a casino.** There is no built-in house edge against you. Technical analysis works because institutions, algorithms, and professional traders all follow the same levels. The reason retail traders lose is not randomness -- it is poor execution, bad psychology, and insufficient preparation.

Come in with a clean slate or you will fight your own assumptions the entire way.

## Capital Requirements & Account Types

### Pattern Day Trader (PDT) Rule
- Accounts under $25,000 are limited to 3 day trades per rolling 5-day period on margin accounts.
- This restriction does not apply to cash accounts (but cash accounts have settlement delays and cannot short stock).

### Margin vs. Cash Accounts

Use this decision matrix:

| Condition | Account Type |
|-----------|-------------|
| Trending/bullish market, above $25K | **Margin** -- full day trading access, swing trades with spreads |
| Choppy/bear market, under $25K | **Cash** -- unlimited day trades within settled funds, can trade volatility |
| 1-share training phase (any market) | **Cash** -- you need to practice day trading, not be locked out by PDT |
| Above $25K in any market | **Margin** -- no reason not to have it; you control whether to use the leverage |

### Capital Amounts

- **$5,000 starting challenge:** Start with $5K on margin. Use your 3 day trades per week and good swing trades. Goal: double it. Take out $5K profit, repeat. Double again, leave $10K, double that. Build to $35K, then start day trading freely.
- **$30,000 minimum for a working day trading account.** With 4x margin that gives $120K in buying power -- enough to run 10 trades a day averaging $75 per winner and hit $10K/month.
- **$50,000 is a comfortable base** for someone aiming to make a full-time living.

### Multiple Account Structure (advanced)

Professional traders typically maintain separate accounts for different purposes:

- **Day Trading account** -- active trading, profit withdrawn monthly, base increased 15% every 6 months
- **Long-term stock account** -- positions from exercised puts, covered calls for income
- **LEAPS account** -- deep ITM calls 1+ year out, selling weekly calls against them (Poor Man's Covered Calls)

You do not need this on day one. Start with one account.

## Stocks vs. Options: When to Use Which

The decision comes down to two variables: **time** and **money**.

**Stocks:**
- No expiration -- you can hold as long as needed
- With a strong daily chart, 90%+ of stock trades eventually become winners if given enough time
- Ties up more buying power
- Use when: you have the account size, the daily chart supports a swing, or you need to be able to wait

**Options:**
- Provide leverage -- participate in large-cap moves with less capital
- Have a ticking clock (time decay) working against you from the moment of purchase
- Require immediate directional movement to be profitable
- Use when: you need leverage due to account size, the stock setup demands immediate movement, or you are using spreads to define risk

**Rules of thumb:**
- Always buy options at least 1-2 weeks from expiration, with a delta of 0.6 or higher (ITM)
- If you do not know *when* a stock will move, use stock. If the move should be immediate and confirmed, options are fine.
- If you had unlimited capital, you would never use options. You use them because of price constraints -- understand that tradeoff.
- Option spreads (CDS, PDS, butterflies) let you reduce time decay and define risk. Learn these before trading naked options.

## Fundamental vs. Technical Analysis

**Short-term trading is 100% technical analysis.** If you are holding a position for hours or days, the company's P/E ratio, earnings guidance, and balance sheet are irrelevant. You often do not even need to know what the company does.

Technical analysis encompasses: price action, candlestick patterns, support/resistance, moving averages, volume studies, relative strength/weakness to SPY, VWAP, and option pricing.

Fundamental analysis is for long-term investing (months to years). The two are not mutually exclusive -- you can invest long-term AND trade short-term -- but they serve completely different purposes and should not be mixed.

**On indicators:** More than half are noise. Keep your charts clean. The working set: SMA 50/100/200 and EMA 8 on the daily; VWAP, EMA 3/8 on the 5-minute; volume with relative volume; Real Relative Strength indicator. That is approximately all you need.

## The 10-Step Getting Started Guide

### Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1-6)

**Step 0 -- Mental Preparation.** Humble yourself. Unlearn your assumptions. Accept the 2-year timeline. This is a career decision on par with going to law school.

**Step 1 -- Choose a Broker.** Use a desktop platform (not mobile-only). ThinkerSwim, Interactive Brokers, or Fidelity. Must support real-time paper trading. Compare fees -- 100 trades/month of commissions adds up. This choice is sticky; you will likely stay with your first broker.

**Step 2 -- Learn.** Read the books. Take the free broker courses. Core reading list:
- *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets* -- John Murphy
- *Options as a Strategic Investment* -- Lawrence McMillian
- *Trading in the Zone* -- Mark Douglas (psychology/mindset)

Paper trade while learning. Set your paper account to the same balance you will actually start with. Master execution of: going long, shorting, buying calls/puts, call debit spreads, put debit spreads, covered calls, butterflies.

**Step 3 -- Technical Analysis.** Learn to read candlestick patterns, identify support/resistance across timeframes, use the core indicators listed above. Do not chase every indicator -- there is no holy grail. Keep charts clean.

**Step 4 -- Journal.** Use TraderSync, Tradervue, or Edgewonk. Upload every trade. Tag setups and mistakes. Analyze: win rate, profit factor, time of day, trade type, stock type. Your journal tells you what is actually working vs. what you think is working.

**Step 5 -- Choose a Scanner.** Find stocks with: relative volume > 1.5, relative strength/weakness to SPY, above prior day's high (or below prior low for shorts), strong daily charts. Learn to set chart alerts at key levels. A good scanner is one of the most important investments you will make.

### Phase 2: Validation (Months 6-18)

**Step 6 -- Choose a Strategy.** Use the Relative Strength/Relative Weakness method taught in the wiki. Do NOT start with scalping or momentum trading low-float stocks -- these are the hardest strategies and the #1 way new traders blow up accounts.

**Step 7 -- Paper Trade to Targets.** Hit these milestones before using real money:
- 3 consecutive profitable months
- Minimum 100 trades in that period
- Win rate of 75% or higher
- Profit factor of 2.0 or higher

**Step 8 -- Trade 1 Share / 1 Contract.** Switch to real money but trade only 1 share per stock trade, 1 contract per option trade. Hit the same milestones again:
- 3 consecutive profitable months
- 75%+ win rate
- 2.0+ profit factor

This is the most frustrating step. You will want to size up. Do not.

### Phase 3: Scaling (Months 18-24+)

**Step 9 -- Set Goals.** Set monthly profit targets based on your win rate, average trades per day, and average profit per trade. Remove profit each month as salary. Leave the base. Increase the base by 15% every 6 months if profitable.

**Step 10 -- Get an Accountant.** Determine: IRS day trader status eligibility, LLC or S-Corp structure, IRA trading implications, tax-loss harvesting. This is your business -- treat the financials accordingly.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Trading too large.** If your P&L causes emotional reactions, your position size is too big. Trade as if you have 1 share until emotions stop driving decisions.
2. **FOMO.** Chasing a stock because others are excited about it. If the chart setup is good, take it. If you are just reacting to chat hype, do not.
3. **Trading your P&L instead of the chart.** Exit when the technical conditions for the trade no longer apply, not when you hit a dollar amount.
4. **Predicting tops and bottoms.** "It can't go any higher/lower" -- yes it can. Follow the charts, not your gut. Counter-trend trading is a top reason new traders lose.
5. **Chasing methods.** Hopping from strategy to strategy every time you hit a rough patch. Master one method first.
6. **Skipping the learning phase.** Depositing money and immediately scalping low-float gappers or buying OTM options on meme stocks. This is why 90% fail.
7. **Following someone else's trades blindly.** You do not know their account size, exit plan, hedging context, or risk tolerance. Use posted trades as a scanner and educational tool, not a signal to copy.

## Paper Trading Practice Regime

1. Trade the same hours as the real market (9:30 AM - 4:00 PM ET).
2. Set your paper account to match your real starting capital -- do not use $1M fake money.
3. Find 2-3 trades per day using your scanner and the RS/RW method.
4. Journal every trade the same day. Tag the setup, note mistakes.
5. At end of each week: group your best and worst trades. Identify patterns.
6. At end of each month: review all 30-40 trades. Examine missed trades and why. Perform Walk-Away Analysis (would holding longer have improved results?).
7. Do not graduate to real money until you hit: 75% win rate, 2.0 profit factor, 3 consecutive profitable months, 100+ trades.

## Key Metrics

**Win rate is the primary metric.** It determines everything else:

| Win Rate | Required Risk:Reward | Difficulty |
|----------|---------------------|------------|
| 80% | 1:1.07 | Achievable with good stock selection |
| 60% | 1:2.75 | 250% harder -- need much larger winners vs. losers |
| 40% | 1:4.0+ | Requires extreme precision; bad streaks devastate accounts |

**Target benchmarks:**
- **75% win rate minimum** before going live (paper trading threshold)
- **80%+ win rate** is the professional target
- **Profit factor of 2.0+** (total winning dollars / total losing dollars)
- **3 consecutive profitable months** as the consistency gate at each phase

The higher your win rate, the lower the required reward-to-risk ratio, the more stable your income, and the lower the chance of devastating losing streaks.
